# 42 SDR Interview Questions & Answers (2026 Guide)

You've got an SDR interview coming up. Maybe it's tomorrow. Maybe it's in an hour. Either way, you need to know what they're going to ask — and more importantly, how to answer without sounding like you memorized a script.

This guide covers every question category you'll face across all interview stages: the recruiter screen, the hiring manager deep-dive, the mock cold call, and the final panel. Each question includes a sample answer written the way a real candidate actually talks — not the way ChatGPT writes a cover letter.

## Recruiter Screen Questions {#recruiter-screen}

The recruiter screen is a 20–30 minute vibe check. They're qualifying you on three things: can you communicate clearly, are you genuinely interested in this specific role, and are you roughly qualified. These are pass/fail — you don't need to be brilliant, you need to not disqualify yourself.

### 1. Tell me about yourself.

This isn't "recite your resume." It's a 90-second pitch that explains why you're sitting in this interview. Lead with where you are now, explain the thread that connects your experience, and end with why this role is the logical next step.

**Sample answer:** "Yeah, so basically I've been in sales-adjacent roles for about two years. I started in customer success at a fintech startup where I was basically doing renewals and upsells — that's where I realized I liked the hunting side more than the farming side. I moved into a BDR role at a smaller SaaS company where I was doing about 80 cold calls a day, booking around 12 meetings a month. Now I'm looking for a team with stronger coaching and a product I'm genuinely excited about — which is why I applied here."

**Why it works:** Specific numbers. Clear narrative thread. Ends with genuine motivation for this particular role.

### 2. Why do you want to work in sales?

They're checking if you actually want this job or if you're applying to everything. The honest answer needs to connect to something real in your experience — not "I'm passionate about revenue."

**Sample answer:** "Honestly, I figured it out during my customer success role. I had this one account where the client was about to churn, and I basically had to re-sell them on the product in a 30-minute call. I saved the account and realized that was the most energized I'd felt at work in months. The problem-solving, the real-time conversation, the fact that the outcome depended entirely on how I showed up — I wanted more of that."

### 3. Why this company specifically?

They want to hear that you've done research beyond reading the "About Us" page. Mention something specific — a product feature, a recent announcement, a market position, or an honest personal connection.

**Sample answer:** "Two reasons. First, I've actually used your product — my last company was a customer and I saw firsthand how the sales team sold to us. The discovery call was really good, and I remember thinking 'I want to learn to sell like that.' Second, I looked at your RepVue scores and the quota attainment is above industry average, which tells me the product sells and the territories are fair."

### 4. What do you know about our product?

Don't recite their homepage. Explain it like you'd explain it to a friend who's never heard of the company. Bonus points if you can articulate their ICP and value proposition.

### 5. Where do you see yourself in 2–3 years?

They want to hear "AE" or "moving up in sales." This isn't the time to mention your startup idea or MBA plans.

**Sample answer:** "In an AE role. I want to learn the full cycle — discovery, demo, negotiation, close. The SDR role is where I build the foundation, and I want to do it well enough that the promotion feels earned, not just tenure-based."

## Your Background & Experience {#your-background}

### 6. Walk me through your resume.

Not a chronological retelling. Pick 2–3 stops that matter, explain what you learned at each, and connect them with transition logic: "I moved because..."

### 7. What's the hardest thing you've ever sold?

Could be a product, an idea, or yourself. The point is to show you understand that selling = persuasion under resistance.

### 8. Tell me about your prospecting experience.

They want to hear channels (phone, email, LinkedIn, video), volume (calls per day, emails per week), and results (meetings booked, pipeline generated).

**Sample answer:** "At my last company I was primarily phone and email. I'd do about 60–70 dials a day and send around 40 personalized emails. My connect rate was about 8% on cold calls, and I was booking around 10–12 qualified meetings a month against a target of 8. I also started experimenting with video prospecting toward the end — it was too early to have great data but the response rate was noticeably higher."

### 9. What CRM and sales tools have you used?

Name them specifically. Salesforce, HubSpot, Outreach, Salesloft, Apollo, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, ZoomInfo, Gong, Chorus. If you haven't used their specific tools, say which you've used and that you learn tools quickly.

### 10. What's your biggest professional accomplishment?

Pick something with a number attached. "Hit 140% of quota in Q3" is better than "I'm a team player."

### 11. Why are you leaving your current role?

Never badmouth your current employer. Frame it as moving toward something, not away from something.

**Sample answer:** "I've learned a lot where I am, but the growth has kind of plateaued. There's no clear path to AE, and I'm ready for a team where the coaching is more structured and the product has stronger market pull. That's what drew me here."

## Sales Skills & Knowledge {#sales-skills}

### 12. How do you handle objections?

Framework: Acknowledge → Ask → Reframe. Don't argue. Don't give up immediately. Get curious.

**Sample answer:** "My default is to pause and acknowledge what they said first — something like 'That makes sense' or 'I hear you.' Then I'll ask a question to understand what's really behind the objection. A lot of times 'we don't have budget' actually means 'I don't see enough value yet.' Once I understand the real blocker, I can reframe around that specific concern."

### 13. Walk me through your ideal day as an SDR.

Show you understand the rhythm: morning prospecting blocks, midday calls, afternoon follow-ups, end-of-day pipeline review.

### 14. How do you prioritize your accounts?

Talk about ICP fit, intent signals, engagement history, and account tiering. Show you don't just spray and pray.

### 15. What's your approach to cold calling?

Structure: opener (pattern interrupt or permission-based), value prop (1 sentence), qualifying question, next step. Keep it under 30 seconds before you ask a question.

### 16. How do you personalize outreach at scale?

This is the key tension in the SDR role. Show you can do research quickly, find a relevant hook (company news, job change, LinkedIn post, mutual connection), and weave it into a template without spending 20 minutes per email.

### 17. What metrics do you track?

Activities (dials, emails, connects), outcomes (meetings booked, show rate), and quality (pipeline generated, conversion rate from meeting to opportunity). Show you think beyond just "number of calls."

### 18. How do you qualify a lead?

Know your framework: BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline), MEDDIC, GPCTBA/C&I, or even just "problem, impact, timeline." Name the one you use and explain why.

### 19. What's the difference between a lead, a prospect, and an opportunity?

Lead = marketing qualified, hasn't been contacted. Prospect = you're actively engaging. Opportunity = qualified, in the pipeline with a real chance of closing.

### 20. How do you handle a gatekeeper?

Don't try to trick them. Be direct, respectful, and brief. "Hi, I'm trying to reach [name] about [specific topic]. Is now a bad time for them?" works better than pretending you're returning a call.

### 21. Sell me this pen. (Or: Sell me our product.)

Don't actually pitch the pen. Ask questions first. "Before I pitch you on this pen, can I ask — what are you currently using to write, and what's frustrating about it?" This shows you understand consultative selling.

## Behavioral Questions {#behavioral}

### 22. Tell me about a time you faced rejection. How did you handle it?

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) but tell it conversationally, not robotically.

**Sample answer:** "Yeah, this was actually at my last company. I had a prospect I'd been nurturing for three weeks — perfect ICP fit, they'd engaged with every email, we had a call scheduled. Then they ghosted. Cancelled the meeting, didn't respond to follow-ups. I could have just moved on, but I sent one last email that was super short — basically said 'Hey, totally understand if the timing changed. Just want to make sure I'm not missing something on my end. Would love your honest feedback.' They replied within an hour, said they'd gone with a competitor but appreciated the approach. I asked what the competitor did differently, took notes, and adjusted my positioning for the next 10 prospects. Three of them converted."

### 23. Describe a time you disagreed with your manager. What happened?

Show coachability. Acknowledge their perspective first, explain your reasoning, show you stayed open, and share the outcome.

### 24. Tell me about your biggest failure.

Be honest. Pick a real failure, not a humble brag. Explain what you learned and what you changed.

### 25. How do you stay motivated during a slow month?

Talk about process over outcomes: "I can't control whether prospects pick up the phone. I can control how many dials I make, how good my research is, and whether I'm improving my talk tracks."

### 26. Give me an example of when you had to learn something quickly.

SDR ramp time is typically 30–90 days. They want to know you can absorb product knowledge, ICP details, and competitive positioning fast.

### 27. Tell me about a time you went above and beyond.

Don't make this about working weekends. Make it about creative problem-solving or helping a teammate.

### 28. How do you handle constructive feedback?

The real answer they want: "I ask for it proactively, not just when it's given." If you can share an example of feedback you initially disagreed with but later realized was correct, that's gold.

### 29. Describe a time you had to work with a difficult colleague.

Keep it professional. Show empathy, communication, and resolution — not that you "won" the argument.

## Company & Industry Knowledge {#company-knowledge}

### 30. What do you know about our competitors?

Name 2–3 competitors. For each, give one strength and one weakness relative to the company you're interviewing at. This shows you understand competitive positioning, which is exactly what you'll need to sell against them.

### 31. Who is our ideal customer?

Describe their ICP: industry, company size, typical buyer title, and the problem they're solving. If you can name a real customer from their website, even better.

### 32. What trends do you see in our industry?

Pick one real trend — AI adoption, budget tightening, consolidation, regulatory changes — and connect it to how it affects their sales motion.

### 33. How would you explain our product to a prospect who's never heard of us?

Keep it to 2 sentences. Lead with the problem, not the product. "You know how [problem]? We fix that by [solution]. Companies like [customer] use us to [outcome]."

## Mock Cold Call Questions {#mock-cold-call}

Most SDR interviews include a live roleplay. The interviewer plays the prospect; you cold call them. This is where 70% of candidates fail — not because they're bad at selling, but because they're not prepared for the format.

### 34. "I'm busy right now."

Don't say "I'll be quick." Say: "Totally understand. Is there a better time this week I can call you back? I'm reaching out because [one-sentence value prop] and I think it might be relevant to [something specific about their company]."

### 35. "We already have a solution for that."

Don't panic. Say: "Makes sense — most companies in your space do. Out of curiosity, what are you currently using? The reason I ask is that a lot of teams using [competitor] have told us [specific pain point]. Is that something you've experienced?"

### 36. "Just send me an email."

This is usually a brush-off. Say: "Happy to. So I can send something relevant — what's the biggest challenge you're dealing with right now in [their area]?" If they actually answer, you've started a discovery conversation.

### 37. "What does your product do?"

Keep it under 15 seconds. Problem → solution → proof point. "We help [ICP] solve [problem] by [mechanism]. Companies like [name] have seen [result]."

### 38. "How much does it cost?"

Don't give pricing on a cold call. Say: "It depends on a few things — team size, use case, and what you're currently spending. Rather than throw out a number that might not be relevant, can I ask a couple questions to see if it even makes sense for us to talk?"

## Questions to Ask Them {#questions-to-ask-them}

Always have 3–5 ready. These signal you're evaluating them too — which is exactly the mindset of a strong salesperson.

### 39. "What does quota attainment look like across the team?"

This tells you if the OTE is realistic. If only 30% of the team hits quota, that $85K OTE is aspirational, not expected.

### 40. "What does the ramp period look like for new SDRs?"

You want to hear a structured plan — not "you'll figure it out." A good answer includes product training, call shadowing, and a protected ramp quota.

### 41. "What's the path to AE, and how long does it typically take?"

Shows ambition without being presumptuous. Listen for specifics versus vague "it depends."

### 42. "What separates your top performers from everyone else?"

This tells you what to optimize for. If the answer is "activity volume," it's a grind shop. If it's "quality of conversations and pipeline conversion," that's a healthier org.
