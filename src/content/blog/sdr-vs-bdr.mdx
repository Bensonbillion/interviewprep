# SDR vs BDR: What's the Difference and How to Interview for Each

Here's the honest answer you won't find on most career sites: the difference between SDR and BDR depends entirely on the company. There's no universal standard. Some companies use the titles interchangeably. Others draw sharp distinctions.

That said, when there IS a difference, it follows a consistent pattern. Understanding that pattern matters for two reasons: it helps you pick the right role, and it helps you tailor your interview answers so you're speaking the hiring manager's language.

---

## The core difference (when one exists)

**SDR (Sales Development Representative):** Focused on inbound leads. Marketing generates interest — webinar signups, demo requests, content downloads, free trial activations — and the SDR qualifies them, follows up fast, and books meetings for Account Executives.

**BDR (Business Development Representative):** Focused on outbound prospecting. No one asked to hear from you. The BDR researches target accounts, writes personalized outreach sequences, makes cold calls, and creates pipeline from scratch.

Think of it this way: an SDR catches fish that swim toward the boat. A BDR goes out with a spear.

**But here's the catch:** At least 40% of tech companies use SDR and BDR interchangeably, or flip the definitions entirely. Salesforce, for example, uses BDR for their outbound role. HubSpot uses SDR. Some companies use ADR (Account Development Representative) for outbound instead.

**What to do:** Read the job description carefully. Ignore the title. Look for keywords like "inbound leads," "cold outbound," "prospecting," or "lead response time" to understand what the actual job involves.

## Day-to-day comparison

| Aspect | SDR (Inbound) | BDR (Outbound) |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Lead source** | Marketing-generated (MQLs) | Self-sourced from TAM |
| **Primary activity** | Speed-to-lead response, qualification calls | Cold calling, email sequences, LinkedIn outreach |
| **Daily calls** | 30–50 (warm/lukewarm) | 60–100+ (mostly cold) |
| **Daily emails** | 20–30 personalized follow-ups | 40–60 personalized cold emails |
| **Key metric** | Lead response time, MQL-to-SQL conversion rate | Meetings booked, pipeline generated |
| **Research depth** | Light (lead already expressed interest) | Heavy (building the case from scratch) |
| **Rejection rate** | Moderate (they came to you) | Very high (you're interrupting them) |
| **Autonomy** | Lower (follow the inbound flow) | Higher (choose accounts, build strategy) |
| **Creativity required** | Moderate | High (personalization is everything) |

## Compensation (real numbers, 2026)

Based on verified data from RepVue (March 2026) for US-based roles:

| | SDR (Inbound-focused) | BDR (Outbound-focused) |
|---|---|---|
| **Median base salary** | $58,000–$65,000 | $55,000–$62,000 |
| **Median OTE** | $80,000–$95,000 | $78,000–$90,000 |
| **Top performer earnings** | $120,000–$145,000 | $115,000–$140,000 |
| **Typical base/variable split** | 70/30 | 65/35 |
| **Quota attainment (industry avg)** | ~57% | ~52% |

SDR comp tends to be slightly higher because inbound leads have higher conversion rates, which means more consistent variable pay. BDR comp is slightly lower on average but the top end can be higher at companies that reward outbound hustle with accelerators.

**The real money difference** isn't SDR vs BDR — it's the company. An SDR at Snowflake earning $105K OTE makes more than a BDR at a Series A startup earning $75K OTE. Company selection matters more than the title on the offer letter.

## Career paths

Both roles lead to the same place: Account Executive. The path looks like this:

**SDR path:** SDR → Senior SDR → AE (typically 12–18 months)

**BDR path:** BDR → Senior BDR → AE (typically 12–24 months)

BDRs sometimes take slightly longer to promote because outbound is harder to hit quota on consistently, but they often get promoted into stronger AE roles because they already know how to prospect — which many promoted inbound SDRs struggle with.

**Other paths from SDR/BDR:**
- **Sales Engineer** (if you're technical)
- **Customer Success Manager** (if you prefer account management)
- **Sales Enablement** (if you love coaching)
- **Sales Management** (SDR/BDR Team Lead → Sales Manager)
- **Revenue Operations** (if you're data-oriented)

## How the interviews differ

This is where it gets practical. Even if the titles are used interchangeably at the company you're interviewing at, the interview FOCUS shifts based on whether the role is inbound or outbound.

### If the role is inbound-heavy (SDR):

**What they'll emphasize:**
- Speed and responsiveness (how fast can you follow up?)
- Qualification skills (BANT, MEDDIC — can you sort good leads from bad?)
- Efficiency (can you handle volume without dropping quality?)
- Product knowledge (inbound leads ask informed questions)
- Handoff quality (AEs will complain if the meetings are unqualified)

**Questions you'll likely face:**
- "A demo request comes in at 4:55pm on Friday. What do you do?"
- "How do you decide if a lead is worth passing to an AE?"
- "Walk me through how you'd qualify this lead." (They give you a scenario)
- "How do you handle a lead who requested a demo but won't pick up the phone?"
- "What does a qualified meeting look like to you?"

**How to tailor your answers:**
- Emphasize speed: "My goal is to respond to every inbound lead within 5 minutes. Research shows the odds of qualifying a lead drop 80% after the first 30 minutes."
- Emphasize qualification: "I'd rather book 8 highly qualified meetings than 15 tire-kickers that waste AE time."
- Show you understand the funnel: MQL → SQL → SAL → Opportunity

### If the role is outbound-heavy (BDR):

**What they'll emphasize:**
- Prospecting creativity (can you break through noise?)
- Resilience (can you handle 90%+ rejection daily?)
- Research skills (do you personalize or spray and pray?)
- Writing ability (email copy matters in outbound)
- Self-motivation (no one is handing you leads)

**Questions you'll likely face:**
- "Walk me through how you'd build an outbound sequence for [target persona]."
- "You have a list of 500 accounts. How do you prioritize?"
- "Write me a cold email to [specific persona] on the spot."
- "How do you handle 3 weeks of outreach with zero responses?"
- Mock cold call (almost always included for outbound roles)

**How to tailor your answers:**
- Emphasize research: "I spend 5–8 minutes per account before any outreach. I look at their LinkedIn, recent company news, tech stack, and any trigger events."
- Emphasize volume + quality: "I can maintain 70+ dials a day while keeping every email personalized to the first sentence."
- Show you've been rejected: "My best month was also the month I got told 'no' the most. I made 1,400 dials, got through to about 100, and booked 14 meetings. The 86 rejections taught me more than the 14 yeses."

## The mock cold call: SDR vs BDR version

**SDR mock call (inbound scenario):** The interviewer plays a lead who filled out a demo request form. You're calling to qualify them and book the meeting. The bar is lower on persuasion (they already expressed interest) but higher on qualification (can you figure out if they're a real buyer?).

**BDR mock call (outbound scenario):** The interviewer plays a cold prospect who has never heard of you. You're interrupting their day. The bar is higher on opening the conversation, handling immediate resistance, and generating enough curiosity to earn 30 more seconds.

Both versions test the same underlying skill: can you have a real conversation, not a scripted monologue?

## How to decide which role to target

**Choose SDR (inbound) if you:**
- Prefer a more structured, process-driven day
- Like responding to demand rather than creating it
- Want consistent variable comp
- Are newer to sales and want a smoother on-ramp
- Prefer qualification conversations over cold outreach

**Choose BDR (outbound) if you:**
- Like building something from nothing
- Are comfortable with high rejection rates
- Want to develop stronger prospecting skills for your AE career
- Are creative with messaging and outreach
- Want more autonomy in choosing your targets

**Choose either if you:**
- Just want to get into tech sales and don't care about the label
- Are more focused on the company, product, and team than the title
- Plan to be an AE within 12–18 months regardless

## The honest take

Here's what nobody writes in these comparison articles: the SDR vs BDR distinction is fading. Most modern sales orgs expect everyone to do some outbound and some inbound. The pure "sit and wait for leads" SDR role is shrinking as marketing budgets tighten and companies expect more self-sourced pipeline from everyone.

The skills that matter in 2026 are the same for both roles: the ability to research quickly, write compelling messages, have genuine conversations on the phone, qualify intelligently, and handle rejection without letting it slow you down.

If you're prepping for an interview in either role, the best thing you can do is understand the specific company's version of the job. Read the description carefully. Ask the recruiter in the first call: "Is this more inbound or outbound?" Then tailor every answer accordingly.
