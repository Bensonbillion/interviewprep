# SALESPREP SYSTEM QUALITY STANDARD
## What every prep kit must produce — for any company, any role

---

## THE BENCHMARK

Two real recruiter screens were analyzed (Klir SDR, HubSpot AE).
The transcripts revealed what interviewers actually ask and what
separates strong answers from weak ones.

The goal is not to generate better HubSpot prep.
The goal is to make the SYSTEM generate at this quality level
for every company, every time.

---

## THE QUALITY TESTS

Run these tests on any prep kit generated by the system.
The company doesn't matter. The tests are universal.

---

### TEST 1: THE NUMBERS TEST

Open any answer card. Find any metric mentioned.

PASS: The answer contains numbers from the user's actual experience.
"I'm closing about 7-8 deals a month at an average of $10K ARR
with a $180K quarterly quota"

FAIL: The answer is vague or generic.
"I consistently close high-velocity SMB deals and exceed quota"

HOW TO RUN:
Generate a recruiter screen kit for a made-up company.
Read the current role / TMAY card.
Circle every number. There should be at least 3.
If there are zero numbers, the system failed.

---

### TEST 2: THE ICP TEST

Open the why-this-company card.

PASS: The answer names who the company sells to specifically.
"You sell to companies with 1-50 employees — founders and operators
who need to grow faster. I've spent the last two years selling to
the same buyer persona at Samsara — operators running field service
companies who need to run more efficiently."

FAIL: The answer talks about the company in the abstract.
"I'm excited by HubSpot's culture and the opportunity for growth"
or "I've always admired what you've built"

HOW TO RUN:
Generate a kit for any company.
Read the why-this-company card.
Ask: does it name the customer type this company sells to?
Does it connect that to the user's background?
If you can swap the company name with a different company and
the answer still works — it failed.

---

### TEST 3: THE STORY TEST

Open any behavioral question card (struggle, rejection,
feedback, exceeded expectations, conflict).

PASS: The answer begins with a specific time and situation.
"In Q3 of last year, I had three consecutive weeks with
zero meetings booked..."

FAIL: The answer describes a process or framework.
"When I'm struggling, I usually go back to the basics and
check what's working for the rest of the team..."

HOW TO RUN:
Generate a kit for any SDR role.
Open the struggle/recovery card.
Check the first sentence. Does it contain a time reference?
Does it contain a specific situation, not a general habit?
If the answer could describe any human's general approach
to adversity — it failed.

---

### TEST 4: THE VOICE TEST

Copy any full answer from a prep kit.
Read it out loud in front of a mirror.

PASS: It sounds like a person talking.
Contractions present. Varying sentence length. Some informality.
Could hear this coming out of a real person's mouth.

FAIL: It sounds like an essay or LinkedIn post.
Uniform sentence length. No contractions. Formal transitions.
"Furthermore," "Additionally," "This demonstrates."

HOW TO RUN:
Generate a kit for any role.
Paste the full answer for TMAY into a text-to-speech tool.
Listen to it. Would you believe a real human said this?
If it sounds like AI wrote it — the voice profile failed.

---

### TEST 5: THE TOOLS TEST (AE ROLES ONLY)

Open the prospecting strategy card for any AE role.

PASS: The answer names specific tools with specific purposes.
"I use Sales Navigator to map the org chart, ZoomInfo to pull
contact data, and Gong to review what's working in my sequences..."

FAIL: The answer is abstract about tools.
"I leverage various sales enablement tools to identify and
research prospects..."

HOW TO RUN:
Generate an AE recruiter screen kit.
Open the prospecting strategy card.
List every tool named. There should be at least 2.
The tools should match what's on the user's resume skills section.
If the tools section of the resume says "Gong, LinkedIn Sales Nav"
but the answer says "various tools" — the system failed to pull
from the resume.

---

### TEST 6: THE SWAP TEST

Take the why-this-company card from any kit.
Replace the company name with a competitor.
Read it back.

PASS: The answer breaks. It refers to specific things
about the original company that don't apply to the competitor.
"You launched the Canadian team in 2022 and you're still
building the playbook — that's exactly the stage I want to
be in..." — this breaks if you swap the company name.

FAIL: The answer still works with any company name.
"I'm excited by the opportunity to grow and make an impact
at a fast-growing SaaS company" — this works for any company.

HOW TO RUN:
Generate a kit for any company.
Read the why-this-company card.
Replace the company name with Salesforce.
If it still makes sense — the answer isn't company-specific enough.

---

### TEST 7: THE ROLE TYPE TEST

Generate two kits:
A) An SDR recruiter screen for any company
B) An AE recruiter screen for any company

PASS: The question sets are different.
SDR kit has: motivation, struggle, qualities, day-to-day
AE kit has: current role metrics, full process, prospecting
  strategy, account ownership, deal volume, inbound/outbound split

FAIL: The question sets are the same or nearly the same.
If both kits have the same cards in the same order,
the role-type split isn't working.

---

### TEST 8: THE JD NUMBERS TEST

Generate a kit where a job description was provided.

PASS: A "know the role" card exists with extracted numbers.
The card shows: quota, deal size, call volume, cycle length,
pipeline mix — pulled directly from the JD text.

FAIL: No such card exists, or the numbers in the card
don't match what's in the JD.

HOW TO RUN:
Paste in any tech sales JD with specific numbers.
Generate a kit.
Check: is there a card called "Know the role" or similar?
Do the numbers on it match the JD?
If the numbers don't appear or appear wrong — JD parsing failed.

---

### TEST 9: THE COMP TEST

Open the comp expectations card for any role.

PASS: The card includes:
- What to say when asked your current OTE
- Your target range
- At least one question to ask about uncapped commission
  or accelerators
- Coaching on what to do if the OTE is lower than current

FAIL: The card only says what number to give.
"My target is $X-Y OTE" with no guidance on the conversation.

---

### TEST 10: THE POWER CLOSE TEST

Open the questions-to-ask card for any recruiter screen.

PASS: The card includes the power close question as a
distinct section labeled something like "ask this last":
"Before we wrap up — are there any concerns about my
background that would give you pause about moving me forward?"

With coaching: "This forces a verbal commitment or surfaces
an objection you can address in real time. Most candidates
never ask it."

FAIL: The power close question is absent, buried, or
presented without coaching on why it works.

---

## HOW TO USE THESE TESTS

Before shipping any prompt change to the system:
Run all 10 tests on 3 different kits:
- One SDR role at a company the system knows well
- One AE role at a company the system knows well
- One role at a completely made-up company with no data

If any test fails on any kit: the system is not ready.
The goal is 10/10 on every kit generated, for any company.

---

## THE ONE-LINE STANDARD

Every answer must be:
SPECIFIC enough that it couldn't describe any other candidate.
COMPANY-SPECIFIC enough that it couldn't apply to any other role.
HUMAN enough that it sounds like it came from a real person.

If it passes those three — it's ready.
If it fails any one — it goes back.
